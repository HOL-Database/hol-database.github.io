In Keoris, where the world is technologically advanced, there is extra equipment and vehicles commonly at your disposal, through purchasing or other means. 

#### Guns 
Guns in Keoris use electric pulses to fire bullets made of elemental energy (most commonly lightning energy).

Guns do not have addons such as different scopes, laser sights, supressors, etc.. This would be too mechanically complex.

###### Weapon Properties 
Some new weapons have additional properties, which will be listed here.
**Spray.** This weapon can be used to make normal attacks, or it can spray a 10-ft cube within range with shots. Each creature in the area must make a Dexterity saving throw against a DC of 8 + your attack bonus, or take the weapon’s damage (roll the damage once, not once per target). To fire the weapon this way, you must expend 10 shots. 
**Repeating.** A weapon with the repeating property shoots several shots in quick succession, which in turn gives a higher chance of hitting a target. When you hit a creature with a gun with this property, you may reroll any number of damage dice up to a number of times equal to the value listed next to the weapon in the table. You must take the last rolled numbers. For each time you reroll a die, you expend one additional piece of ammunition. 
**Scope.** If a weapon has the scope property, you can use your action to give yourself advantage on your attack rolls on your next turn with that weapon. You also don’t suffer disadvantage from attacking at a further distance. 
**Special (Flare Gun).** A flare gun shoots flares instead of bullets. After shooting, a flare stays on the ground, lighting an area with bright light around it to a distance of 20 feet, and dim light an additional 20 feet beyond that. Flares cost 2 silver each. 
**Special (Minigun).** You cannot attack with the minigun until you use an action to turn it on. After the fact, you can use the attack action on each of your turns for one minute, after which the minigun is overheated and is unable to be used. After 10 minutes, you can use the minigun again. _**Special (Shotgun).**_ Shotguns shoot a spray of fragments of a bullet. When you attack with the shotgun, each creature in a 20 foot long, 5 foot wide line must succeed on a Dexterity saving throw (DC = 8 + Proficiency Bonus + Dex) or take the weapon’s damage.

###### Ammunition 
Guns in Keoris require bullets to fire. They are made of solid, elemental energy, and can be used in any type of gun. The following types of bullets can deal different damage types, as listed next to them.

**Common Bullets** - costs 1gp for 40: Fire, lightning, piercing, poison, slashing

**Uncommon Bullets** - costs 1gp for 10: Acid, cold

**Rare Bullets** - costs 10gp for 10: Force, Necrotic


#### Armour
The campaign uses normal armour in D&D, but they have different names, such as a chain shirt being renamed to something such as graphene vest, but these changes are purely cosmetic.

###### Shields
There are several extra shields in Keoris. The normal shield in the PHB is considered a “basic shield,” with no special benefits. There are other shields listed below:

**Arm Shield.** This shield attaches to your forearm, and it keeps your hand somewhat free. It gives you +1 to AC instead of +2, but you can hold a weapon in the same hand as the shield. That weapon has disadvantage on all attacks you make with it as long as it’s not light
**Riot Shield.** This shield is about 3 feet tall, with a small window in it. It's meant to hold people back, and it grants you the normal +2 to AC. Additionally, As a bonus action, you can go on the defensive until your next turn. While defending, creatures cannot pass through your space unless they are incorporeal, creatures behind you that are within 5 feet of you gain half cover, and you get an additional +1 to AC.

#### Vehicles
In Keoris, there are vehicles such as trucks, cars, motorcycles, and other vehicles. There are flying vehicles but they are restricted to cities that have a flight grid, which allows said vehicles to fly, and low-altitude crafts that fly at most 30 metres above the ground. Since such vehicles are costly or use a lot of magic, most vehicles have wheels. 
Vehicles all have an AC, movement speed, and hit points, and those with wheels have special rules for movement, such as quick turns and tumbling, listed below.

**Quick Turning.** Vehicles have limited range of turning, and as such are less mobile in combat. If a character driving a vehicle takes a tight turn, or risky maneuver (something that will be determined through common sense by the whole table), they must succeed on a Wisdom check (DC decided by the DM), with which you are proficient with if you are proficient with Modern Vehicles. On a fail, the vehicle will ram into something, possibly dealing damage to the vehicle and/or the rammed object. In the case of failing the check from a turn, the vehicle will tumble. Motorcycles ignore the quick turning rule.
**Tumbling.** If a vehicle tumbles, each creature must succeed on a Dexterity saving throw (DC decided by the DM), or take 1d8 bludgeoning damage + an amount equal to how much they failed the save by. The vehicle is then considered prone, and cannot right itself unless a creature uses its action to right it with a successful Strength (Athletics) check.

###### Aircrafts
Aircrafts are different from planes. They use a modified and extended variant of the *levitation* spell, which only grants a set amount of lift off of the ground. That combined with jet engines allows them to skim across the landscape, with their maximum height off the ground reaching about a kilometre. 

#### Prosthetics
Prosthetics are a common replacement for replacing limbs instead of using healing magic. They are cheaper, and they can be customized and modified. The downside is they are harder to repair than normally healing organic limbs, but they can be repaired with magic or tools. I might decide if you take enough damage that a prosthetic might be broken. To successfully fix a single broken prosthetic, or attack a prosthetic, you must take one hour of time (which can be taken during a short or long rest) and succeed on an Intelligence check with Tinker’s Tools (Or the mending spell, which you don’t add your proficiency with, but it can replace the tools if you don’t have any). You gain advantage on this check if you have proficiency in Tinker’s Tools and know the mending spell. Below is the list of prosthetics and the detriments you get if they are broken or destroyed.

**Prosthetic Arm.** This prosthetic replaces one of your arms, and acts the same as a normal one. If the arm is broken, you have disadvantage on attack rolls with weapons in that hand. If you have two prosthetic that are both broken, you have disadvantage on spell attack rolls as well. A destroyed arm acts as a lost arm. Spellcasters can use prosthetic arms as spellcasting focuses. Possible upgrades are things such as a flashlight-hand, increased pulling and pushing power, bonus to grappling, etc.
**Prosthetic Leg.** Having a prosthetic leg replaces a leg, which acts as normal. If it is broken, your walking, climbing, and swimming speed (if any) is halved, and if you take the Dash action, you fall prone at the end of the move. Possible upgrades include increased walking speed, increased jump distance, etc., but only if both (or all four in the case of centaurs) are prosthetic.
**Prosthetic Wing.** A prosthetic wing replaces a normal wing, acting as normal. A broken wing halves your flying speed, and ranged attacks have advantage against you while you are in the air. Possible upgrades include increased flying speed and increased AC.
**Other Prosthetics.** There are more complicated/smaller prosthetics that don’t necessarily grant bonuses or detriments, such as prosthetic fingers or a spine. They are just for flavour and have no effect ingame.

Upgraded prosthetics are called [[Cybernetics]]. Cybernetic units can be found on their own page, as they have their own ruling akin to attunement slots with magic items.


#### Other Equipment
There are other, miscellaneous types of equipment and weapons, which will be listed here.

**Gauntlet and Knuckle Type-Weapons.** Weapons that you hold in your hands or have on your forearms that increase your unarmed attacks, like gauntlets and knuckles (brass knuckles for example) are considered weapons, but attacks you make with them are considered both unarmed and weapon attacks. The fact they are weapons allows them to be magical, and make unarmed attacks considered magical attacks.
**Modern Explorer’s Pack (10 gp).** This version of the explorer’s pack has more modern equipment. Includes a backpack, sleeping bag, mess kit, a flashlight (60ft cone, bright the first 30, dim the second 30, 8 hours on a cell) and two power cells, 10 days of rations, and 50 feet of nylon rope, along with a multi-tool with a simple screwdriver, knife, scissors, can opener, and serrated knife.
**Basic Grenade (3 gp).** A simple explosive that detonates shortly after primed, to be thrown at foes. When you use an action to throw the grenade, make a ranged weapon attack at a space within 20 feet of you, or within 60 feet at disadvantage. On a 15 or lower, you throw the grenade to a random space within 5 feet of that area; on a 10 or lower, 10 feet. Then, the grenade explodes, and each creature within a 20 foot sphere of that space must succeed on a DC 13 Dexterity saving throw or take 2d8 bludgeoning damage.  
**Tracker (100 gp).** A small, advanced piece of technology that can be stuck onto surfaces. You can look at your mini-computer and see the precise location and distance of the target, provided they are within 10 miles of you. Otherwise, the tracker shows the general direction of the target. After a tracker is used, it cannot be used again.
