Goukurg is the god of Storytelling, Travel, and Trade. He used to be the primary god of [[Sikurg]] from before the [[Timeline of Keoris History|Protected Age]], but due to his domains, worship of him grew to many countries when the divine barriers were constructed by the Aegis Gods to help create and sustain Keoris.

##### Culture
Belief in Goukurg is relatively simple and hands-off. These days he is only really worshipped by traders and historians, and as such his physical presence is contained to small shrines scattered about the lands. Though, he is often referenced in anecdotes and expressive curses, being the god people either attribute their luck to, either good or bad. This is because he is often referenced as the watcher of every individual's story, even though he has never had a say in fate.

A proverb originating from his culture is as follows:
"Remember, your life is a river"
There are several variations of the saying, but all have the thought that one's future can be predicted, just like walking of a river, but it is never reliable, and you can only see so far ahead. There can be great changes to the river as you travel, but it always keeps on going. As such, rivers are sacred to Goukurg.

Goukurg has always encouraged creating fictional stories as well as recording history, as they can inspire people just the same, and it shows devotion and creativity in storytelling.

He is also the god of trade. Primarily from exchanging stories between travelers, but it has also grown to trading goods between individuals or even towns and countries. However, he is not the god of riches. Goukurg has never had anything to do with material wealth.

> [!note] Religion Structure
> 
>  **Devotee Hierarchy**
> ----------------------
>  There is no properly structured hierarchy to Goukurg, though there are still those that dedicate their lives to him. These people are called Monks of Adage. They keep records of stories and legends told either through penmanship or by word of mouth in archives. Each story has two copies -- one for safekeeping in the inner sanctums of the archives, and another to be cycled around with other Monks and their archives.
>
>  **Places of Worship**
>----------------------
>
>  **Shrines**
Shrines dedicated to Goukurg are small wooden canopies filled to the brim with writing supplies. They are often built near a river for individuals to give offerings. Offerings to Goukurg are always small snippets of one's day written on water soluble paper and thrown into the river. During colder weather, there is a pickaxe or drill supplied to break through the ice.
>
>  **Archives of Adage**
Devotees to Goukurg, the Monks of Adage run and upkeep archives that keep records of stories and legends told either through penmanship or by word of mouth in archives. Each story has two copies -- one for safekeeping in the inner sanctums of the archives, and another to be cycled around with other Monks and their archives. There are rarely more than half a dozen monks per archive.

##### History
Goukurg has barely any history aside from being one of the Aegis Gods that brought up the barriers. He as always been a passive god, taking joy in watching people's lives, whether good or bad. Most stories that include him are tall tales describing him granting a hero the key to defeating an evil villain. Usually told to children.
